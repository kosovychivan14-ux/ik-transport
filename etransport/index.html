WHERE THE MONEY COMES FROM

Four types of transport —
four rental markets

Each of these formats already operates in major cities around the world. Here is how the rental works — and what the open market data says.

01

Electric bikes

Rented by the minute or by the day — for city rides, couriers and tourists. The simplest format for entering the rental business.

MARKET NUMBERSTourist and storefront operators charge $50–80 per day for a single e-bike, and the average 30-minute shared e-bike ride in the US costs $14.30 (US industry data).

02

Electric scooters

The most mass-scale format of city rental: a rider pays about $1 to unlock and $0.30–0.39 for every minute of the ride.

MARKET NUMBERSWell-managed fleets receive $300–800 per scooter per month in revenue. The key factor is utilisation: from 3 rides a day per scooter.

03

Driverless cars

Robotaxis drive themselves and take paid orders with no driver. The model follows the fleet-operator pattern: partners own and maintain the cars, while the platform provides the technology — this is how the big robotaxi services scale through ride-hailing platforms and fleet operators.

MARKET NUMBERSRobotaxis complete over 500,000 paid rides a week across 15 US cities — this is no longer testing, it is a working business.

04

Delivery drones

Drones deliver food, medicine and parcels faster than couriers: a typical delivery by a major US retailer takes about 23 minutes from the order. Drone operators work together with major platforms and retail chains.

MARKET NUMBERSA major US retailer has passed 1 million drone deliveries, the world's largest drone delivery operator has completed over 2.5 million deliveries worldwide, and a major online retailer has announced a plan to expand to nearly 500 US cities by the end of 2026.

Figures are from open sources and industry reviews from 2025–2026. They are market reference points, not a promise of a specific result: the outcome depends on the format, the city and the participation terms.

THE MATH OF ONE DEVICE

How the rental result is calculated —
step by step

A transparent calculation using the electric scooter as an example — the most mass-scale rental format. All figures below are industry market estimates from open sources, not a promise of income.

STEP 1 · RIDES

People rent the device: about $1 to unlock and $0.30–0.39 per minute of the ride. To cover its own costs, a device needs to make from 3 rides a day.

STEP 2 · REVENUE

Well-managed fleets receive $300–800 per scooter per month in revenue. The revenue level depends on the city, the location and how busy the device is.

STEP 3 · MINUS COSTS

The revenue pays for charging, repairs, insurance and permits. Most of the revenue goes exactly to keeping the fleet working every day instead of standing idle.

STEP 4 · NET RESULT

After all costs, the industry's typical net margin is 10–20% of revenue. This is the modest but real economics of rental transport.

STEP 5 · DISTRIBUTION

The net result is shared between the participant, the fleet's operating needs and the partner fund. The exact distribution terms are shown openly in the registration bot — before you start, not after.

THE POOL PRINCIPLE

The result comes from the whole fleet,
not from a single device

Your result does not depend on the fate of one scooter or one bike. It is formed by the work of the entire fleet of devices — that is the pool principle.

  • One device is charging or being repaired — the rest keep carrying people and earning
  • Downtime of a single unit does not stop the work of the whole pool
  • The exposure is spread across many devices and several types of transport

QUESTIONS & ANSWERS

The essentials,
in short

How is the rental result of one device calculated?+

According to industry market estimates: a device earns on tariffs — about $1 to unlock and $0.30–0.39 per minute. Well-managed fleets receive $300–800 per scooter per month in revenue, and after the costs of charging, repairs, insurance and permits, the net margin is typically 10–20%. These are market reference points, not a guarantee of a specific result.

What is the pool principle?+

The result is formed by the work of the entire fleet of devices, not one specific device. If one device is being repaired or charged, the rest keep working — so the downtime of a single unit does not stop the pool.

What happens if a device breaks down or is stolen?+

Every unit is GPS-tracked, the equipment is insured, there is a reserve fund for repairs, and a device that goes out of service is replaced with a working one. That is why a single breakdown does not stop the whole fleet.

How does a “Passive Participant” differ from a “Partner”?+

A passive participant takes part only in the transport rental — no team and no recruiting people. A partner additionally builds their own team within E-Transport and takes part in the partner fund. The exact terms of both formats are shown in the registration bot when you sign up.

Is the income guaranteed?+

No. There are no income guarantees and there cannot be any: the figures on this page are industry market estimates, and the real result depends on the city, the season and how busy the devices are.
